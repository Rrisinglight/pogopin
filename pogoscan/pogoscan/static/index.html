What do Noise check, Calibrate, Zero and Re-init do?
Noise check
Reads 50 conversions (about 1 s at 50 Hz) from whatever the pin touches right now, records nothing, and reports the mean and the scatter (standard deviation) in µV. The scatter tells you how healthy the setup is: about 0.6 µV is the chip's own noise at gain 128, so that is the best you can get per conversion. Much more means pickup (mains hum, a moving cable), a poor or oxidised contact, or an open input. Exactly 0 means a stuck line. The mean is just the present DC level; with the pin on the reference block it shows the offset that Zero would remove. Averaging 100 conversions per point reduces the 0.6 µV to roughly 0.06 µV.
Calibrate
Runs the AD7705's self-calibration: the chip disconnects the pins, shorts its own inputs to measure its zero error, then measures full scale against its 2.5 V reference, and stores both corrections in its offset and gain registers (about 0.2 s, no measurement possible meanwhile). It corrects only errors inside the chip, which drift by about 0.1 µV per °C. It runs automatically when a scan is created or opened and after every automatic re-init; press it by hand after a large temperature change or when readings on the reference block drift.
Zero
Corrects what Calibrate cannot: offsets outside the chip, such as thermal EMF in the pogo pin, the wires and the connectors. Put the moving pin on the reference contact block (same metal as the reference, so the true difference is zero), press Zero, and the page shows every value minus that reading. Raw values stay in the file; "relative to zero" switches the display. Re-zero when the fixture temperature changes.
Re-init ADC
Hardware reset of the chip, reprogramming of gain 128 / 50 Hz / bipolar, then a self-calibration. The server does the same automatically when it finds the chip back at its power-on registers (after a supply blink) or after a DRDY timeout; the button is for doing it deliberately after fixing a supply or wiring problem.
