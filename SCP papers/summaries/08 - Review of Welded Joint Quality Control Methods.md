## Abstract

*(Translation of the authors' own abstract.)*

The article describes the methods of welded-joint quality control existing today and their essence. It examines the visual method, the method of radiographic transillumination of welded joints, the ultrasonic method, the kerosene-test method, the magnetographic method, the method of fluorescent and dye-penetrant flaw detection, the chemical-reaction method, and the method of hydraulic testing.

## Keywords

Inspection methods (методы контроля), welded joints (сварные соединения), defects (дефекты).

## 1. Visual inspection (external examination)

Any inspection of welded joints begins with an external examination, by means of which it is possible to reveal not only external defects but also some internal ones [1-4]. For example, differing height and width of the weld seam and unevenness of the ripple folds indicate frequent breaks of the arc, whose consequence is lack of fusion.

**Surface preparation before examination.** The seams are thoroughly cleaned of slag, scale and metal spatter. More thorough cleaning — in the form of washing the seam with alcohol and etching with a **10 % nitric acid solution** — gives the seam a matt surface on which fine cracks and pores are easier to notice. After the acid has been used, it must not be forgotten to remove it with alcohol to avoid corrosive attack on the metal.

**Defects revealed.** Visual inspection of welded joints reveals, first of all, external defects: geometric deviations of the seam (height, width, fillet leg), external pores and cracks, undercuts, lack of fusion, and overlaps.

**Aids.** For effective inspection, supplementary local illumination and a magnifier of **5–10× magnification** are used. The magnifier is a very useful instrument in this case: it helps to reveal many defects that cannot be examined with the naked eye — fine hairline cracks emerging at the surface, burnt metal, barely visible undercuts. It also makes it possible to track how a particular crack behaves in service — whether it is growing or not.

**Measurement.** During external examination, measuring instruments are also used to gauge the geometric parameters of the welded joint and of the defects: a vernier caliper, a ruler, and various templates/gauges [5-8].

## 2. Radiographic transillumination of welded joints

This method is based on the ability of X-rays or gamma rays to penetrate through the thickness of the metal, acting on a sensitive photographic film, photographic paper, or a selenium plate applied to the seam from the opposite side. At places where there are pores, slag inclusions or lack of fusion, darker spots are formed on the film (plate).

**Detection capability.** X-ray transillumination reveals defects in metal **up to 60 mm thick**, of size **0.5–3 % of the metal thickness**; gamma transillumination — in metal **up to 100 mm thick**, of size **2–5 %**.

**Stated blind spots.** Transillumination does not permit the detection of cracks if they are located at an angle of **not more than 5° to the direction of the central beam**, and likewise does not reveal lack of fusion in the form of sticking-together of the welded metals **without a gas or slag interlayer**.

**Escalation procedure on finding defects.** If impermissible defects are found in a seam, a **doubled number** of seams (joints) is transilluminated. If defects are found again, **all** seams welded by that particular welder are transilluminated. The defects revealed are removed, the seams are re-welded, and they are transilluminated again.

**Reference radiographs.** For assessing seam quality it is recommended to have reference radiographs with characteristic defects for thicknesses of **8–12, 14–20, 30–50 and 60–100 mm**. Albums of reference radiographs are approved by the inspectorate of **Gosgortekhnadzor** and by the administration and are an inalienable part of the technical specifications for the acceptance of products.

## 3. Ultrasonic method

The ultrasonic method is based on the differing reflection of a directed beam of high-frequency sonic oscillations (**0.8–2.5 MHz**) from the metal (the weld seam) and from the defects present in it in the form of discontinuities [9-12]. It is applied to the inspection of weld seams in steels and non-ferrous metals.

**Instrumentation chain.** To obtain ultrasonic waves, piezoelectric plates of **quartz or barium titanate** are used, which are inserted into holder-probes. The reflected oscillations are picked up by a search unit, converted into electrical pulses, fed to an amplifier, and reproduced by an indicator.

**Acoustic coupling.** To ensure acoustic contact, the surface of the article at the point of inspection is abundantly covered with oil (**autol grades 6, 8, 18; compressor oil, and so on**).

**Limiting sensitivity** (expressed as the smallest detectable defect area):

- metal thickness **up to 10 mm** — **0.2–2.5 mm²**;
- **over 10 up to 50 mm** — **2–7 mm²**;
- **over 50 up to 150 mm** — **3.5–15 mm²**.

## 4. Kerosene test

One of the simplest and most accessible methods is the kerosene test. It serves to determine the density (leak-tightness) of weld seams on metal **up to 10 mm thick**. The kerosene test detects defects of size **0.1 mm and above**.

**Procedure.** The side of the seam accessible for examination is covered with an aqueous suspension of **chalk or kaolin** and dried. The opposite side is wetted **2–3 times** with kerosene. Permeability is detected by greasy yellow spots on the surface covered with chalk or kaolin.

**Hold times and acceleration.** The duration of the test is **not less than 4 h at positive temperature** and **not less than 8 h at a temperature below 0 °C**. If the seams are heated to **50–60 °C**, the process is accelerated to **1.5–2 h**. It can also be accelerated by blowing the seams with compressed air from the kerosene side, or by creating a vacuum.

## 5. Magnetographic method

Magnetographic inspection is based on the detection of **leakage (stray) fields** that are formed at the locations of defects when the inspected articles are magnetized. The leakage fields are recorded on an **elastic magnetic tape** pressed tightly against the surface of the seam [13-15]. The recording is made on a flaw detector, or it is read off.

**Defects revealed.** Surface and sub-surface macrocracks, lack of fusion, pores and slag inclusions **at a depth of 2–7 %** in metal **4–12 mm thick**.

**Defects revealed less distinctly.** Pores of rounded shape, wide lack-of-fusion zones (**2.5–3 mm**), and transverse cracks whose direction coincides with the direction of the magnetic flux.

**Verification and rate.** In a number of cases the results of magnetic inspection are verified by transillumination. The productivity of the method is **5–6 m/min**.

## 6. Fluorescent (luminescent) and dye-penetrant (colour) flaw detection

The principle of operation consists in introducing into the cavity of the defect a **fluorescing solution** or a **bright-red penetrating liquid**, which is subsequently removed from the surface [16-18].

**Fluorescent variant.** Under the action of **ultraviolet rays**, a visible glow occurs from the solution adsorbed out of the defect cavity.

**Colour variant.** In dye-penetrant flaw detection, defects are revealed with a **white developing paint** (against the white background there appears a red pattern corresponding to the shape of the defect).

**Scope.** With the help of these methods, surface defects are revealed — chiefly cracks in various welded joints, including those made of **non-magnetic steels, non-ferrous metals and alloys**.

## 7. Density inspection by the chemical-reaction method

The essence of the method is that a **4 % phenolphthalein solution** is applied to the outer seam of metal **up to 16 mm thick**, or gauze impregnated with a **5 % silver nitrate solution** is laid onto it. Air mixed with **ammonia** is pumped into the article (the ammonia is obtained from a cylinder of liquefied gas).

**Indication.** At places of local leaks the phenolphthalein is coloured **bright red**, and the silver nitrate **silvery-black**.

**Preparation.** Before the test, thorough cleaning of the seam from contamination and slag is required.

## 8. Hydraulic (pressure) testing

The hydraulic test is carried out by filling with water in order to test the **strength and density** of vertical tanks, gasholders and other vessels with a wall thickness of **not more than 10 mm**.

**Filling test.** The water is poured in to the **full height of the vessel** and held for **not less than 2 h**.

**Jet test.** The welds of open vessels are subjected to a jet from a hose with a fire nozzle (**outlet orifice diameter 15–30 mm**) at a pressure **not below 1 atm (0.1 MPa)**.

**Test with additional hydrostatic pressure.** The additional pressure is created in a closed vessel filled with water by means of a **head (pressure) tube of diameter not less than 30 mm**, and also by a hydraulic pump. The magnitude of the pressure is determined according to the technical specifications and the rules of **Kotlonadzor**.

**Mechanical stimulation and evaluation.** While the tests are being conducted, the weld seams are tapped with a hammer of mass **0.5–1.5 kg**. Defective places are identified by the presence of **drops, trickles of water, and sweating (condensation film)**.

## Key numerical values and conditions

**Visual inspection**
- Etching solution for surface preparation: **10 % nitric acid**, followed by alcohol removal.
- Magnifier: **5–10× magnification**.

**Radiographic transillumination**
- X-ray: metal **up to 60 mm** thick; defect size **0.5–3 % of the thickness**.
- Gamma: metal **up to 100 mm** thick; defect size **2–5 %**.
- Angular blind spot for cracks: **not more than 5°** to the direction of the central beam.
- Escalation on impermissible defects: transilluminate a **doubled** number of joints; on repeat findings, **all** seams of that welder.
- Reference-radiograph album thickness bands: **8–12, 14–20, 30–50, 60–100 mm**.

**Ultrasonic method**
- Frequency band: **0.8–2.5 MHz**.
- Piezoelectric plate materials: **quartz, barium titanate**, mounted in holder-probes.
- Couplant: **autol grades 6, 8, 18; compressor oil**.
- Limiting sensitivity: **0.2–2.5 mm²** (up to 10 mm); **2–7 mm²** (over 10 to 50 mm); **3.5–15 mm²** (over 50 to 150 mm).

**Kerosene test**
- Metal thickness: **up to 10 mm**; minimum detectable defect: **0.1 mm**.
- Developer: aqueous suspension of **chalk or kaolin**, dried; kerosene applied **2–3 times** on the opposite face.
- Hold time: **≥4 h** at positive temperature; **≥8 h** below 0 °C; **1.5–2 h** if seams are heated to **50–60 °C**.

**Magnetographic method**
- Detected defect depth: **2–7 %**; metal thickness **4–12 mm**.
- Poorly resolved wide lack of fusion: **2.5–3 mm**.
- Inspection rate: **5–6 m/min**.

**Chemical-reaction method**
- Metal thickness: **up to 16 mm**.
- Indicators: **4 % phenolphthalein** solution, or gauze soaked in **5 % silver nitrate** solution.
- Test gas: **air mixed with ammonia** from a liquefied-gas cylinder.
- Colour response: phenolphthalein → **bright red**; silver nitrate → **silvery-black**.

**Hydraulic testing**
- Vessel wall thickness: **not more than 10 mm**.
- Water held at **full vessel height** for **not less than 2 h**.
- Hose fire-nozzle outlet diameter: **15–30 mm**; jet pressure **not below 1 atm (0.1 MPa)**.
- Head (pressure) tube diameter for additional hydrostatic pressure: **not less than 30 mm**.
- Tapping hammer mass: **0.5–1.5 kg**.

## Conclusions (as stated by the authors)

The article has no separate conclusions section; the text ends with the description of hydraulic testing. The authors' own summarizing statements, given in the abstract and opening paragraph, are:

1. The article sets out the welded-joint quality-control methods existing today together with the essence of each: the visual method, radiographic transillumination, the ultrasonic method, the kerosene test, the magnetographic method, fluorescent and dye-penetrant flaw detection, the chemical-reaction method, and hydraulic testing.
