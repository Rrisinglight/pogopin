## Abstract

The article is devoted to the improvement of non-destructive testing (NDT) of metal machine parts by the contact potential difference (CPD) method. It presents the results of experimental studies of the influence of environmental parameters on the measurement of contact potential difference with a Kelvin probe on the surface of metallic specimens made of Al, Ti and Ni. The dependence of the results of CPD measurements on metal surfaces upon the parameters of the surrounding environment is explained. Practical recommendations for measuring contact potential difference on the surface of parts are given. The results obtained are applicable in the manufacture, operation and repair of metal machine parts.

## Keywords

Electron work function, contact potential difference, Kelvin probe, metal, machine parts.

## 1. Introduction: surface damage of metal parts and the CPD method of non-destructive testing

Improving the methods of non-destructive testing of metal parts (MP) of machines in order to maintain a prescribed level of their reliability is a topical task. Structural alloys based on Al, Fe, Ti, Ni, Cr and other metals have found wide application in mechanical engineering, in particular in the aerospace industry. Metal parts and assemblies of aircraft and spacecraft are operated under severe conditions, at the limit of their strength properties. They are acted upon by considerable forces and moments, extreme temperatures and pressures, corrosive media and so on. Some parts — for example the turbine blades of gas-turbine engines — operate at temperatures above the melting point of the material from which they are made. Under such service conditions in aviation and space technology it is precisely the *surface* of metal parts and assemblies that takes up the maximum loads [1–5].

As a result of service conditions, and at times also through violations of manufacturing, operating or repair technology, defects are formed on the surface of metal parts. Surface and sub-surface discontinuities of the material are dangerous, especially cracks in metal parts, which act as stress concentrators and are capable of leading to fracture of the part. Timely detection and elimination of dangerous defects in metal machine parts is an important task.

A promising method is known — the contact potential difference (CPD) method, which belongs to the electrical class of non-destructive testing of the surface of metal parts [6]. The CPD method is applied in mechanical engineering for investigating the properties of the surface of metal parts: in tribological studies, in assessing the quality of cleaning of their surface from contaminants before gluing, soldering and welding of parts, in monitoring the quality of the application of protective coatings, and so on.

The CPD method makes it possible to determine the magnitude of the electron work function (EWF) of a metal or alloy. The contact potential difference method is based on comparing the work function values of the metal under investigation and of the metallic measuring electrode (ME) of the CPD measuring instrument.

The work function is a fundamental parameter of a solid. From its level one can judge the energy characteristics of metallic specimens and the state of their surface. **An increase in the measured CPD indicates a decrease in the electron work function of the metals.**

To determine the electron work function of metals, the dynamic (vibrating) capacitor of a Kelvin probe is used, which permits CPD to be measured; in it the measuring electrode of the instrument's sensor is set into oscillatory motion. In the CPD measuring instrument "Poverkhnost-11" («Поверхность-11») [7] used by the author, the measuring electrode is made of pure Ni with an electron work function of about $8.0109 \times 10^{-19}$ J, and its oscillation frequency is 410 Hz. When the sensor of the instrument is applied to a metal part, its oscillating measuring electrode and the surface of the metal under investigation form a dynamic capacitor in which a contact potential difference arises, and this is registered by the instrument.

Literature sources [8–12] mention the fact that the atmosphere influences the determination of the electron work function by the CPD method. However, [8] states that at present **no quantitative theory of the temperature dependence of the electron work function exists**.

## 2. Temperature dependence of the electron work function and the CPD of metals

Temperature, the most important thermodynamic parameter of a solid body, exerts an influence on the magnitude of the electron work function and the CPD of metals. The temperature dependence of the CPD was investigated as long ago as by A. Volta. His first law states: *"At the contact of two different metals a potential difference arises which depends on the chemical nature and on the temperature of the junctions."* However, to this day the information on the temperature dependence of the electron work function and the CPD of metals is contradictory. The temperature dependence of the electron work function may be either direct or inverse, and depends in large measure on the conditions under which it is determined [8].

In practice, an example of the realisation of the temperature dependence of the CPD is provided by capacitive temperature-measuring sensors. By analogy with such sensors, the Kelvin probe of the "Poverkhnost-11" instrument, when applied to the surface of a metal part, likewise forms a capacitor — only a dynamic one. The temperature dependence of the CPD arising in the dynamic capacitor is based on the change of the capacitance $C$ as a consequence of the change of the relative permittivity $\varepsilon$ of the medium in the gap between the surfaces of the metal part and the measuring electrode of the Kelvin probe, according to the formula

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon S}{d} \tag{1}$$

where
- $\varepsilon_0$ — the electric constant (permittivity of free space), F/m;
- $\varepsilon$ — the relative permittivity of the medium in the gap between the measuring electrode and the surface of the metal under investigation; for dry air $\varepsilon \approx 1.00059$ (dimensionless);
- $S$ — the area of the measuring electrode of the CPD measuring instrument's sensor, m²;
- $d$ — the gap between the measuring electrode and the surface of the metal under investigation, m.

During non-destructive testing of metal machine parts, a contact potential difference $U$ arises in the capacitor of the Kelvin probe; it is related to the capacitance of the capacitor $C$ by the formula

$$U = \frac{q}{C} \tag{2}$$

where
- $q$ — the electric charge of the capacitor, C (coulombs);
- $C$ — the capacitance of the dynamic capacitor, F;
- $U$ — the contact potential difference, V (reported throughout the experimental part in mV).

Formulae (1) and (2) together supply the author's explanatory mechanism used throughout the paper: any environmental parameter that raises $\varepsilon$ raises $C$ and therefore *lowers* $U$, and conversely.

## 3. Influence of atmospheric humidity on the electron work function and the CPD of metals

In [11] it was shown that determination of the electron work function of specimens of Al, Zn and W by the CPD method in a humid atmosphere entails a *decrease* of the CPD. The cause of this is the water vapour in the air, which brings a negative charge; this charge passes partly into the metal and then remains on the metal surface in the water layer. During a subsequent measurement of the CPD on the surface of the metals in dry air, the water evaporates from the surface of the specimens. The contact potential difference of the metals thereby increases, which indicates a decrease of the electron work function of those metals.

By analogy with capacitive temperature sensors, an example of the practical realisation of the dependence of the electron work function and the CPD upon air humidity is provided by the capacitive sensors of moisture-meter instruments. The contact potential difference in the dynamic capacitor of the Kelvin probe changes as a consequence of the change of the relative permittivity $\varepsilon$ between the surfaces of the metal part and the measuring electrode according to formula (1). As the humidity of the surrounding medium increases, the permittivity $\varepsilon$ of the gap of the dynamic capacitor — and consequently its capacitance $C$ — in the general case increase according to formula (1). The CPD of the metals $U$ thereby decreases according to formula (2). This indicates an increase of the electron work function of the metals as a consequence of an increase in the humidity of the surrounding air atmosphere.

## 4. Influence of atmospheric air pressure on the electron work function and the CPD of metals

The influence of air atmospheric pressure on the electron work function and the CPD of metals was investigated in [11]; it was established that at different atmospheric pressures the CPD of metals changes insignificantly. However, almost always the CPD of pure metals at an air pressure of $1 \times 10^5$ Pa is *smaller* than at a pressure of $1 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa. For specimens made of metal azides, by contrast, the reverse processes were observed: their CPD decreased as the air pressure decreased. The change of the CPD is explained by the difference in the adsorption–desorption processes of atoms and molecules of donor-type and acceptor-type gases on the surface of the metallic specimens.

Thus there exist theoretical and practical grounds for a quantitative assessment of the influence of the environmental parameters — temperature, relative humidity and air pressure — on the non-destructive testing of metal machine parts by the CPD method.

## 5. Aim of the work

The aim of the present article is to investigate the influence exerted on the measurement of the contact potential difference of metals by the principal parameters of the surrounding air atmosphere: temperature, relative humidity and pressure. The present article is a continuation of the investigations whose results are set out in [13].

## 6. Investigation of the influence of environmental parameters on the measurement of the CPD of metals

### 6.1. Specimens, instruments and reference conditions

The experimental investigations were carried out in a laboratory, under atmospheric conditions close to normal, on **flat specimens of Al, Ti and Ni having a chemical purity of 99%**. By normal atmospheric conditions the author understands a **temperature of 20 °C, a relative humidity of 58% and an air pressure of 1013 hPa**. Temperature, relative humidity and air pressure were evaluated by means of a **"Hama EWS-800" digital weather station with a declared measurement accuracy of 2%**.

Over the several months during which CPD measurements were carried out on the Al, Ti and Ni specimens, the range of environmental parameters determined by the "Hama EWS-800" weather station was:

1. temperature — from **14 to 29 °C**, with a mean value of **23 °C**;
2. relative air humidity — from **19 to 59%**, with a mean value of **34%**;
3. atmospheric pressure — from **963 to 1022 hPa**, with a mean value of **997 hPa**.

### 6.2. Specimen preparation and measurement procedure

Before the CPD measurement, the flat Al, Ti and Ni specimens were prepared by the method described in [12] (RF Patent 2488093, 2013 — a method of preparing the surface of metal parts for the rapid detection of grinding burns by measurement of the electron work function). The contact potential difference of the specimens was measured with the "Poverkhnost-11" instrument.

In total, **314 CPD measurement sessions** were performed on the specimens. Each session consisted of **eight CPD measurements** of the specimens. From these eight measured CPD values the **minimum and maximum values were discarded**, and from the **remaining six** measured CPD values their arithmetic mean was computed, and it was this value that was taken into account. Such a procedure is necessary in order to exclude gross errors ("outlying" values) in the measurements and to raise the accuracy of the CPD measurements, since the electron work function and the CPD are inherently subject to fluctuation.

### 6.3. Statistical results of the measurements

The results of the CPD measurements were processed by the methods of mathematical statistics. A preliminary check of the measurement results showed that their distribution is close to normal. The following statistical results were obtained for the CPD measured on the surface of the specimens:

1. for **Al** — arithmetic mean CPD $\bar{U}_{\mathrm{Al}} = 874$ mV, with a standard deviation of the CPD $\sigma = 75$ mV;
2. for **Ti** — arithmetic mean CPD $\bar{U}_{\mathrm{Ti}} = 284$ mV, with a standard deviation of the CPD $\sigma = 70$ mV;
3. for **Ni** — arithmetic mean CPD $\bar{U}_{\mathrm{Ni}} = 208$ mV, with a standard deviation of the CPD $\sigma = 65$ mV.

The values of the standard deviations $\sigma$ of the CPD measured on the surface of the metallic specimens indicate the magnitude of the scatter of the CPD $U$ relative to the estimates of their mathematical expectation (the arithmetic mean values) $\bar{U}$.

The contact potential difference $U = 208$ mV between the nickel specimen under investigation and the likewise nickel measuring electrode of the Kelvin probe of the "Poverkhnost-11" instrument is explained by the difference in their manufacturing technology and in the parameters of their surfaces — above all roughness — which exerts a definite influence on the energy state of their surfaces.

### 6.4. Regression analysis

In order to determine the magnitude of the influence of the environmental parameters on the CPD of the Al, Ti and Ni specimens, a **linear regression analysis** of the results of the experimental investigations was carried out using the **"Statistica 10"** program. The regression analysis shows the influence of changes in the values of the predictors — temperature, relative humidity and air pressure — on the dependent variable, namely the CPD of the metals under investigation. Table 1 presents the results of the regression analysis: linear equations that, **with a confidence probability of 0.95**, describe the influence on the CPD $U$ of the Al, Ti and Ni specimens of changes in temperature $t$, relative humidity $h$ and air pressure $p$. Table 1 also presents the values of the **multiple correlation coefficients $R$**, which indicate the adequacy of the linear regression equations obtained.

The three regression equations, written out in full, are:

$$U_{\mathrm{Al}} = 1113.6591 - 6.2083\,t - 3.3569\,h + 0.0162\,p, \qquad R = 0.54$$

$$U_{\mathrm{Ti}} = -67.1834 - 14.2041\,t + 0.5297\,h + 0.6606\,p, \qquad R = 0.50$$

$$U_{\mathrm{Ni}} = 47.0687 - 9.477\,t + 1.6381\,h + 0.3235\,p, \qquad R = 0.41$$

where
- $U$ — the contact potential difference of the corresponding metal specimen, mV;
- $t$ — the air temperature, °C;
- $h$ — the relative air humidity, %;
- $p$ — the atmospheric air pressure, hPa;
- $R$ — the multiple correlation coefficient (dimensionless), characterising the adequacy of the linear regression equation.

## 7. Discussion of the results of the investigations

Analysis of the regression equations (Table 1) shows that **the greatest influence on the CPD of the metals under investigation is exerted by temperature**. With an increase of temperature by 1 °C, the CPD of the specimens decreases: **Al by 6 mV, Ti by 14 mV and Ni by 9 mV**, respectively. The influence of relative humidity and of air pressure on the CPD of the metals, however, is very weak. The multiple correlation coefficients $R$ of the linear regression equations (Table 1) indicate a weak joint influence of the environmental parameters on the CPD, especially for specimens made of Ni.

Notwithstanding the fact that the influence of the environmental parameters on the CPD is weak, it nevertheless exists. The decrease of the CPD $U$ with an increase of the ambient *temperature* $t$ is likewise explained by the decrease of the permittivity $\varepsilon$ with increasing temperature according to formula (1). The permittivity of air $\varepsilon$ decreases for the reason that, as temperature increases, the air becomes more rarefied and there is a smaller number of air atoms in the gap of the dynamic capacitor conducting electric current between the measuring electrode and the metallic specimens under investigation, in accordance with formula (1).

The difference in the dependences of the CPD $U$ (Table 1) on the *relative air humidity* $h$ — inverse for Al and Ti, and direct for Ni — may be explained by the different adsorption processes on their surfaces during interaction with water. For specimens of Al and Ti this interaction is of the **acceptor** type (leading to a decrease of the CPD), whereas for specimens of Ni it bears a **donor** character, ultimately leading to an extremely weak increase of the CPD.

It must be noted, however, that the CPD measurements took place in a relatively dry air atmosphere (the mean measured value of relative humidity amounted to 34%). Higher air humidity exerts a greater influence on the CPD [14].

The increase of the CPD $U$ with an increase of the *atmospheric pressure* $p$ (Table 1) is explained by the increase in the number of air atoms transmitting electric charges in the gap of the dynamic capacitor, in accordance with formula (1). However, such an increase of the CPD is extremely small. **Under normal environmental conditions, the influence of atmospheric pressure on the CPD and the electron work function of metals may, in all probability, be neglected.**

When measuring the CPD at extremely low atmospheric pressure — for example at high altitude — the CPD of metals may decrease more intensively. All the more, the factor of the influence of air pressure on the CPD of metals must be taken into account when performing CPD measurements at artificially reduced pressure (for instance in vacuum) or, on the contrary, at elevated atmospheric pressure, for example when air is being force-fed.

## 8. Conclusions

*(Section «Выводы» of the original; reproduced as stated by the author in the section "Conclusions (as stated by the authors)" below.)*

The experimental investigations showed that under laboratory conditions a change of the environmental parameters does not exert a substantial influence on the measurement of the CPD of metals. The weak dependence of the CPD of metals upon the environmental parameters is explained by the physico-chemical properties of the metals and by the adsorption processes occurring on their surface.

However, the influence of the surrounding environment on the CPD of metals may be more substantial when the CPD is measured under conditions differing significantly from normal — at high or low values of air temperature and humidity, in vacuum or in a rarefied atmosphere, and so forth. In any case, significant deviations of the ambient temperature from the normal value must be taken into account during CPD measurements of metals.

The magnitude of the CPD of metals changes when it is measured under non-stationary environmental conditions, when at least one parameter — temperature, relative humidity or air pressure — changes [13].

The experimental investigations and calculations carried out confirmed the information that, of the three metals investigated, **Ni shows the minimum dependence on environmental parameters**. For this reason Ni is used for manufacturing the measuring electrodes of CPD measuring instruments [7].

It must be remembered that the surface of the metallic specimens under investigation has to be carefully cleaned of contaminants before the CPD measurement — for example by the method set out in [12] — since contamination of the metal surface significantly influences the results of the CPD measurements.

Thus, in order to raise the accuracy of the CPD measurement of metal parts, it is recommended that measurements be carried out **in a laboratory, under normal conditions, and under atmospheric conditions that do not change during the process of CPD measurement**. If it is impossible to observe these recommendations, then **corrections must be introduced into the measurement results using the coefficients of the environmental parameters from the equations presented in Table 1**. The results obtained may be applied in the non-destructive testing of metal machine parts during their manufacture, operation and repair. This will make it possible to increase the effectiveness of non-destructive testing of metal machine parts by the CPD method, with the aim of maintaining a prescribed level of their reliability.
